# Trusted-group MVP: cut drafts, sharing, moderation, and progression

The MVP is played within groups of people who already know each other — a safe space. This removes the machinery that existed to manage strangers, public stakes, and outward distribution.

## Cut for MVP

- **Draft** and the edit/grace window — a **Jumper** submits directly; there is no private-composition status and no post-submission edit window.
- **Share** and all outward distribution — no deep links, no postcard/preview cards, no guest or non-**Account** visitors. Play happens inside a known group.
- **Moderation apparatus** — **Removed Jump** and **Disqualified Jump** are gone (they were enforcement mechanisms for public stakes).
- **House Rules** — cut entirely. Among trusted friends the group self-polices; there is no stated or enforced safety boundary in the domain.
- **Progression / economy** — **Mission**, **Action Mission**, **Level**, **Bounty**, **Sponsored Bounty** are out (the #314 brief lists these as Scope OUT — premature progression/economy layers).
- The **public feed** concept — there is no feed; there are only **Rounds** inside a known group.

## Why

The owner's organizing principle for the MVP is "a safe space among people who already know each other." Most of the cut machinery only earns its keep when the audience includes strangers or the stakes are public. Cutting it yields the smallest possible domain and the most stable contract, consistent with the goal of an API that does not change often.

## Known risk

The #314 brief flagged **House Rules** as load-bearing "because **Transgression** incentives can escalate." Cutting it leaves the escalation concern with no domain anchor. This is an accepted MVP risk given the trusted-group scope; it is the most likely item to be reintroduced if the product ever opens beyond known groups.
