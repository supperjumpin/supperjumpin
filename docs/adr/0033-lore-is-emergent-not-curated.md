# Group lore is emergent and derived, not a curated collection

Durable group memory ("Lore") is modeled as an **emergent, derived view** computed from **Reaction** density across **Rounds** — not as a first-class aggregate node that players write to, and not via an explicit "canonize this moment" action.

There is **no nominate/vote/canonize verb** in the domain. The reveal surface carries no Lore UI; the **Recap** is where emergent standouts resurface.

## Why

Two independent expert analyses (anthropology of group memory; party-game mechanic design) converged: in real friend groups, lore is created by repetition and successful callbacks — the group's laughter does the canonizing. Any explicit act is post-hoc *ratification of an already-hot moment* ("save this"), never *conferral of status* ("vote this funny"). A forced nominate-to-canon step damages felt authenticity (it makes the tacit explicit, dissolving the in-group boundary) and, at the reveal, suppresses the spontaneous reaction flow the product depends on (a stamp that reads as a ballot gets tapped less).

The starboard pattern (react → threshold → auto-surface) gives the *feeling* of agency without a curation step, because canon-ness is a derived consequence of genuine reactions.

## Constraints this locks in

- **Lore is moment/quote-keyed and community-owned**, never per-**Player** attributed. A per-player canon tally would silently rebuild the competitive **Standings** that the #314 pivot pushed backstage. This keeps **Lore** distinct from **Award** (a reserved v2 per-player concept).
- The reaction-density threshold and recap presentation are **edge/tunable surfaces**, not contract spine.
